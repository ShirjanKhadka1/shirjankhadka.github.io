Setup

One security, one date range, one strategy at a time.

    Risk

    Fixed-fractional position sizing on every trade.

    How a position is sized.
    Risk amount = risk % × current equity. Stop distance = entry price − (entry − stop × ATR14). Shares = floor(risk amount / stop distance), rounded down to whole shares and never more than cash allows. Example: Rs 100,000 equity, 1% risk, Rs 500 entry, ATR14 of Rs 20, 2× stop → stop distance Rs 40 → floor(1000 / 40) = 25 shares, Rs 12,500 at risk of Rs 1,000.

    Trading costs

    Real Nepali brokerage costs, applied to every simulated trade.

    CGT rates were revised in September 2026 and can change; confirm the current rate with your broker.

    What gets charged.
    Broker commission per side: up to Rs 50,000 at 0.36% (minimum Rs 10); Rs 50,001 to 500,000 at 0.33%; Rs 500,001 to 2,000,000 at 0.306%; Rs 2,000,001 to 10,000,000 at 0.27%; above Rs 10,000,000 at 0.243%. SEBON fee 0.015% on buys and sells. DP charge Rs 25 on every sell. Name transfer Rs 5 on every buy. Buy-side costs enter your cost basis; CGT applies only to positive taxable gains. The equity curve is net of all of this, so the final point is what you would actually take home.

    The exact rules.

    No hidden logic. Signals use only data available at that day's close, and trades execute at that close.

    Buy and hold

    1. Buy as many whole shares as the starting cash allows at the first close of the range.
    2. Hold everything to the last close, then sell.
    3. No stops, no targets, no sizing: the honest baseline.

    SMA 20 / 50 crossover

    1. Buy at the close when SMA 20 crosses above SMA 50.
    2. Exit at the close when SMA 20 crosses below SMA 50.
    3. Exit early at the ATR stop or target, whichever the day's range touches first (stop wins ties).
    4. One position at a time; a new entry needs a fresh cross.

    RSI(14) swing

    1. Buy at the close when RSI(14) falls below 30.
    2. Exit at the close when RSI(14) rises above 70.
    3. ATR stop and target apply the same as above.

    Long NEPSE backtests found this style loses money: oversold often stays oversold here. Run it and see for yourself.

    What this does not do.

    • Daily closes only. Intraday movement is ignored except for checking whether the day's high/low touched the stop or target.
    • No brokerage commission, DP charges, or capital gains tax are modeled. Real trading costs money on every leg.
    • No slippage: fills are assumed exactly at the close, stop, or target price.
    • Dividends, bonus shares, and right issues are not adjusted for; price drops on ex-dates will look like losses.
    • Only securities with full OHLC history can be simulated. LTP-only listings are excluded, never faked.
    • A profitable backtest does not predict future results. Markets change, and this market especially rewards humility.
    Educational use only. Simulations run on historical data with simplified assumptions and are not investment advice. Nothing here is a buy or sell recommendation; past simulation results do not predict future performance.
