Citizens Bank proposes 4% dividend: 3.80% bonus shares plus 0.20% cash

The cash portion is not extra income. At exactly one-nineteenth of the bonus, it covers the 5% tax on the bonus shares, so the bonus lands tax-free in shareholder accounts.

What did the board actually propose?

The board of CIZB Citizens Bank International, meeting on 6 Ashoj 2083, proposed distributing 3.80% bonus shares and a 0.20% cash dividend from the paid-up capital. The proposal goes to the 20th annual general meeting on 28 Ashoj for shareholder approval, and needs the usual regulatory nod before the bonus shares are listed.[1]

Why is the cash dividend exactly 0.20%?

Because it is arithmetic, not generosity. Bonus shares attract a 5% tax, and the cash that funds it is itself taxed at 5%, so the cash a company must declare is the bonus divided by nineteen: 3.80 / 19 = 0.20. This season's declarations sit exactly on that line, which tells you the cash exists only to settle the tax bill. Shareholders receive the bonus shares effectively tax-free.

Who gets the dividend, and by when?

The bank will close its books for one day on Ashoj 19 for the AGM and dividend. Shareholders holding shares bought on NEPSE up to Ashoj 16 will be eligible to attend the AGM and receive the dividend. Buy on or after Ashoj 17 and you miss this round.[1]

What else is on the AGM agenda?

Beyond the dividend, the meeting will authorize the board to amend the Articles of Association as regulators require, and appoint the auditor for FY 2083/84 on the Audit Committee's recommendation, with remuneration to be fixed. Standard housekeeping, but the auditor appointment is worth noting for anyone tracking governance.

Why it matters

  1. The headline "4% dividend" is really 3.8% in shares. The 0.20% cash only settles the tax, it is not spendable income.
  2. The ratio is formulaic this season: cash equals bonus divided by nineteen. Any declaration sitting exactly on that line pays no real cash.
  3. Eligibility has a hard cutoff. Your purchase must settle by Ashoj 16; the book closes on Ashoj 19.
