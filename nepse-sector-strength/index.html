Sector Strength

Every sector, scored. For each of four windows — 7, 21, 45 and 90 calendar days — a sector earns a strength score from how far its return sits above or below the NEPSE index, stretched across the board so the strongest sector reads 100 and the weakest 0. A risk score rewards calm: 100 × (1 − the sector's volatility ÷ the board's highest). One identity holds on every row: overall = 0.7 × mean strength + 0.3 × risk. Relative strength, window by window

How far each sector's return sits from the NEPSE index's return on the selected window, in percentage points. Positive bars beat the index; negative bars trail it. The index's own returns are printed in the methodology so every bar can be re-derived by subtraction.

    Does strength stick?

    Measured, not asserted: this exact board replayed on weekly snapshots over the trailing three years, each snapshot compared with the same board 28 calendar days later.

    A rank correlation near +1 would mean next month's board is this month's board; near 0 means the ranking reshuffles. These are descriptions of the past three years, not forecasts — read the board as a snapshot of where strength sits today, not where it will sit next month.

    Sector readings

    How this board is built

    The construction. Windows are calendar days: for a window of N days, the base is the last close on or before the date N days before the latest session. A sector's return is its derived index's close-to-close return across the window; relative strength is that return minus the NEPSE composite's return over the identical window. The benchmark's own returns on this board: —. The strength score min-max normalises the relative strength across the 11 sectors, per window — the strongest sector is 100, the weakest 0, by construction, on every window.

    Risk. Volatility is the population standard deviation of the sector index's daily percentage returns over the trailing 60 sessions. The risk score is 100 × (1 − volatility ÷ the board's maximum volatility), so the board's most volatile sector scores 0 and calmer sectors score higher. The level bands the volatility itself: Low below 1.5, Moderate below 2.5, High above — our banding, stated here rather than hidden.

    Overall and phase. Overall = 0.7 × the mean of the four strength scores + 0.3 × the risk score; sectors rank by overall. The phase is the quadrant of two signs: the 90-day relative strength (long end) and the 7-day strength score at or above 50 (short end) — Leading (both strong), Improving (long weak, short strong), Weakening (long strong, short weak), Lagging (both weak).

    Momentum labels. Each window's label bands that window's own sector return: +5% or more Strong Bullish, +1% or more Mild Bullish, between −1% and +1% Flat, −1% or lower Mild Bearish, −5% or lower Strong Bearish.

    The sectors. Each sector is a derived index built by Nepse Decode: the equal-weighted chained daily returns of the sector's listed companies, computed from our own OHLC archive (the same series behind the Seasonality, Market Regime, Sector Rotation and Sector–Symbol Relation pages) — not the official cap-weighted NEPSE sub-indices, so levels differ from cap-weighted sources by design. Sector membership is today's map applied historically, so delisted names are absent — a survivorship caveat we state rather than hide. Our taxonomy has 11 sectors: Trading companies are folded into Others, and Hotels And Tourism is its own sector.

    Checked against the market. This construction reproduces the shape of the reference sector-strength board computed from the official sub-indices: on the shared 2026-10-16 session the two boards' relative strengths rank-agree at 0.99 (7-day) to 0.84 (90-day), the overall scores at 0.86, and 9 of the 10 sectors present on both boards land in the same phase. The gaps are the equal-weight basis above — membership and weighting — quantified here, not smoothed over.

    Reading it honestly. A strength score is a relative, backward-looking statistic: someone is always 100 and someone always 0, even in a week when every sector fell. It is not a quality grade, not a valuation, and not a promise about the next window; the persistence numbers above show how much of today's ranking survives a month.
