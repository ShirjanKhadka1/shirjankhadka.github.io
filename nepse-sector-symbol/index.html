      How this board is built

      The construction. Windows are calendar days. For a window of N days, the base is the last close on or before the date N days before the latest session. A stock's return is its close-to-close return across the window; its sector return is the return of its sector's derived index over the identical window; the relative return is the difference, in percentage points. The sector returns printed here are the same figures the Sector Price Rotation board publishes for the shared windows — one sector series feeds both pages.

      The sectors. Each sector is a derived index built by Nepse Decode: the equal-weighted chained daily returns of the sector's listed companies, computed from our own OHLC archive (the same series behind the Seasonality, Market Regime and Sector Rotation pages) — not the official cap-weighted NEPSE sub-indices, so levels differ from cap-weighted sources by design. Sector membership is today's map applied historically, so delisted names are absent — a survivorship caveat we state rather than hide. Our taxonomy has 11 sectors: Trading companies are folded into Others, and mutual funds are an instrument type rather than a sector index.

      Coverage. — Stocks without a close on both sides of a window are listed as unscored for that window rather than dropped silently: —

      Stability. The stability table replays this exact board on weekly snapshots across the trailing three years of our archive and derived indices, comparing each board with the same board 28 calendar days later. It is measured history, published so the board is read with the scepticism its own record earns.

      Forward tracking. Every published board's top-decile leaders — the stocks furthest ahead of their sector on each window — are recorded the day the board first appears, and resolved 28 calendar days later: the board is recomputed at the last session on or before board+28d, and a leader counts as still ahead if its relative return is still positive. Sign persistence is measured the same way over every stock present on both boards. Boards are never reconstructed backwards: tracking started with the 2026-10-16 board, and earlier boards are not backfilled.

      Refreshing. This page re-checks for a new board every five minutes during market hours, every thirty minutes otherwise, and every two hours on weekends; it only re-renders when a new board has actually landed, and your window, sector, sort and band selections survive the refresh. The board itself is rebuilt after every close — during market hours the page honestly shows the last close's board, and the session line above says which session that is. A status line under the session line reports the board session, the stability snapshots tracked, and the last refresh check. Your current view is shareable: the address carries the window, sector, sort and band.

      Paging. The relation board shows 25 stocks at a time with Prev and Next and a "Showing X–Y of Z" line; filtering by sector, band or search, and changing the sort, returns you to the first page. The sector-stocks block pages the same way, and returns to page 1 when the sector, window, sort or search changes.

      Reading it honestly. A relative return says a stock moved more or less than its sector over a past window — nothing more. It is not a quality grade, not a valuation, and not a promise about the next window; the stability numbers above show how often the relation simply flips.
