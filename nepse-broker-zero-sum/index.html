Broker Zero-Sum Analysis

Every share bought through one broker was sold through another. Add up every broker's net position in a stock and the total is exactly zero — the market is a closed transfer. What that leaves visible is the interesting part: which brokers absorbed the stock and which brokers supplied it. This board maps that transfer for every traded stock — the accumulator and distributor cohorts, their gross flows and average rates, the net flow at seven windows, and the complete per-broker net table — computed from our own floorsheet archive, in which the zero-sum identity is verified for every stock before anything is published.

    How this board is built

    The tape. Every figure comes from Nepse Decode's own floorsheet archive — the complete NEPSE floorsheet (every trade, with its buyer broker and seller broker), collected daily after the close once the day's tape is final. Broker data is transaction flow — what moved through each broker — never a record of what any investor holds.

    The zero sum. For a stock and a window, each broker's net is their bought quantity minus their sold quantity. Because every trade has one broker on each side, the nets across all brokers cancel to exactly zero, and total buys equal total sells in both shares and rupees. The builder verifies that identity for every stock at every window before publishing; a day that fails it does not ship. (Not every published floorsheet elsewhere passes this test — the complete tape does.)

    The cohorts. The accumulator cohort is the top decile of net-buying brokers by net size (a tenth of the side, rounded up, at least one); the distributor cohort is the same on the net-selling side. This is Nepse Decode's published rule — the equivalent commercial board does not disclose its membership rule; ours was fitted against its published cohorts (agreement recorded in our teardown notes) and is stated here so anyone can recompute it. A cohort's flow is its members' gross side flow — everything the accumulators bought, everything the distributors sold — and the net flow is accumulators' gross buys minus distributors' gross sells. A positive net flow reads ACCUMULATING, a negative one DISTRIBUTING. Stance follows the sign of the cohort net in shares; the rupee net shown beside it can rarely differ in sign (different average rates on the two sides), and those stocks are shown with their share stance and their rupee net side by side. Totals here are broker-net sums (buy minus sell), single-counted — never halved, never doubled. The sector rank is the stock's position inside its sector by that side's cohort flow.

    Windows are calendar days ending at the latest session: 1, 7, 15, 30, 90, 180 and 365 days. Coverage: the floorsheet archive starts 2026-07-15, so the 180- and 365-day windows cover the archive only and are labelled partial; the symbol-level rows for 2026-10-07 were not retained (broker totals only), so windows reaching that date rest on the sessions the archive holds — the session count is printed with every window.

    The forward record replays the 30-day stance at weekly snapshots across the archive and measures each stock's return over the next 10 sessions from final, corporate-action-adjusted closes — no lookahead. It is a short, single-era sample and is published as description, not prediction.
