An index moves because market caps move. This board prices that arithmetic for the latest session: every scored stock's index points — its shares × its price change, divided by the official index divisor — ranked from the biggest push to the biggest drag, with its weightage, volume and turnover beside it. The sector table's contributions, plus one printed residual row, sum exactly to the official NEPSE move. Whatever this board cannot explain stays in that residual, with its causes — never netted away, never hidden.

    How this board is built

    The construction. A capitalisation-weighted index moves when market caps move. Each scored stock's contribution is shares × (close − previous close) ÷ divisor, where the divisor is the official one: NEPSE's total market capitalisation divided by the index level — a near-constant Rs 1.72 billion per point that the archived official snapshots pin down session by session (it steps only when listings or corporate actions change the market's capital base). Sector contributions are the sum of their stocks' points; sector contributions plus the printed residual sum exactly to the official move. An earlier version of this board divided by its own covered universe instead — with only ~92% of the market scored, that inflated every contribution by the missing share and left a −3.59 point gap on 16 October 2026. The model was rebuilt on 24 October 2026; the same session now reconciles to +0.09.

    Share counts. Shares come from three sources, in order: paid-up capital in the latest published quarterly filings compilation, divided by the Rs 100 par; the same identity paid-up carried per stock in this site's ownership archive; and, for the few ordinary shares both compilations miss (Nepal Reinsurance and Bishal Bazar among them), the total implied by that archive's measured float — float shares ÷ (1 − promoter %), ownership as of 19 July 2024. The filings counts were cross-checked against the archive: no scored name's count disagrees beyond rounding. Counts refresh at quarterly compilations, so a bonus or right issue allotted mid-quarter enters this board's weights at the next compilation.

    Prices. Final revised closes from Nepse Decode's own archive — the adjusted series where it covers a stock (it also absorbs the mechanical ex-date price drop the way the official divisor does), the raw traded closes otherwise. The distinction matters: the raw archive's 14 October 2026 bars turned out to be a preliminary vintage — over half differ from the final closes — and pricing a board off them made the next session's moves run about half a percent hot market-wide.

    The residual. The official index also counts listed names this board cannot score — ordinary shares with no share count in either source, newly listed names not yet in any compilation (BENH, listed 15 October 2026, is the current example) and the promoter-share listings outside the ordinary-share basis — and NEPSE adjusts its own divisor for listings and corporate actions. Their net move, plus those adjustments, is the residual printed in the sector table. Debentures and mutual funds are not constituents of the NEPSE equity index and are excluded from this board entirely. The residual is part of the board, not an error term to be tuned away.

    The live board. Between 11:00 and 15:00 on trading days this page shows the session in progress, rebuilt every five minutes from the live quote feed: each stock's last-traded price stands in for its close, the divisor and the weightages are carried from the previous session's official state (the divisor is a within-day constant), and volume and turnover are cumulative for the session so far. A live board is marked LIVE and provisional, with the quotes' as-of time beside the badge — last-traded prices are revised at the close on some names, so a live board's figures can differ slightly from the final ones. After the close, the final board — priced on final revised closes — replaces it on this same page, and the final board is the one that enters the record below. Outside market hours the page shows the last final board.

    Refreshing. This page re-checks for a new board every five minutes during market hours, every thirty minutes otherwise, and every two hours on weekends; it only re-renders when a board has actually changed — a new session, a newer quotes as-of time, or the live board giving way to the final one — and your sector tab and symbol search survive the refresh. A status line under the session line reports the board session, the mode (LIVE provisional with its quotes as-of time, or FINAL), and the last refresh check, including failed checks.

    Paging. The full board shows 25 stocks at a time with Prev and Next and a "Showing X–Y of Z" line; changing the sector tab or the search returns you to the first page. The sector-stocks block pages the same way.

    The stop rule. If the residual ever exceeds ±2.5 index points, or the scored board's coverage of the official total market capitalisation falls under 85%, the daily build fails instead of publishing a board we cannot stand behind. The ±2.5 tolerance is set from the rebuilt model's validation across the five sessions before this fix — residuals +0.72, −0.08, −0.57, −0.37 and +0.09, against +0.60, +0.09, −2.61, −4.67 and −3.59 under the old model — roughly three and a half times the worst residual the rebuilt model has produced.

    Reading it honestly. Points measure weight, not merit: the biggest contributor is usually simply a heavy stock that rose, and the biggest detractor a heavy stock that fell. One session's board is a snapshot of who carried the index today, not a judgement on any company.
