NEPSE Contributors

An index moves because market caps move. This board prices that arithmetic for the latest session: every scored stock's index points — its shares × its price change, divided by the board's divisor — ranked from the biggest push to the biggest drag, and rolled up by sector. Then it does the part most boards skip: it adds its own rows up and prints the result next to the official NEPSE move. Whatever this board cannot explain is printed as the gap, with its causes — never netted away, never hidden.

        How this board is built

        The construction. A capitalisation-weighted index moves when market caps move. Each scored stock's contribution is shares × (close − previous close) ÷ divisor, where the divisor is the scored universe's total market cap at the previous close divided by the official index's previous close. Sector points are the sum of their stocks' points, and the board's rows sum to the move it explains — every figure on this page can be re-derived from the printed shares, closes and divisor.

        Share counts. Shares are paid-up capital from the latest published quarterly filings compilation, divided by the Rs 100 par — the same share counts the filings' own EPS figures divide into. Counts refresh at quarterly compilations, so a bonus or right issue allotted mid-quarter enters this board's weights at the next compilation.

        Prices. Raw traded closes from Nepse Decode's own OHLC archive — the basis the official index itself uses. On a stock's ex-date for a bonus or right issue, the official divisor absorbs the mechanical price drop on the day; this board's filings-based share count absorbs it only at the next compilation, so an ex-date can leave a one-day mark in the gap.

        The gap. The official index also counts listed symbols this board cannot score — companies whose paid-up figures are not in the filings compilation (mostly small caps), promoter shares and preference shares — and NEPSE adjusts its own divisor for new listings and corporate actions. Their net move, plus those adjustments, is the gap printed above. It is part of the board, not an error term to be tuned away.

        The stop rule. If the gap ever exceeds ±7.5 index points, or the scored board's coverage of the official total market capitalisation falls under 85%, the daily build fails instead of publishing a board we cannot stand behind — the same tolerance stated in the builder that produces this page.

        Reading it honestly. Points measure weight, not merit: the biggest contributor is usually simply a heavy stock that rose, and the biggest detractor a heavy stock that fell. One session's board is a snapshot of who carried the index today, not a judgement on any company.
