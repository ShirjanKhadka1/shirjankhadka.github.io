Fundamentals

The company behind the chart

The classic ratios, in plain language.

Eight ratios analysts read in annual reports, explained briefly. Expand to read.

Where a company has published quarterly figures, its real P/E, EPS, net profit and NPL appear in the table and snapshot above. Ratios that need full annual statements are explained here as concepts only. Nothing is estimated.

P/E · Price to Earnings

Market price divided by earnings per share. Roughly: how many years of current earnings you pay for with the share price. High P/E means the market expects growth; it can also mean the price is stretched.

P/B · Price to Book

Market price divided by book value per share. Compares what you pay to the accounting value of the company's net assets. Below 1 can mean cheap or troubled; above 1 prices in future value.

EPS · Earnings Per Share

Net profit divided by shares outstanding. The per-share slice of profit. Rising EPS over years is the core of most fundamental stories; one good year is not a trend.

BVPS · Book Value Per Share

Shareholders' equity divided by shares outstanding. The accounting net worth per share. Slow, steady BVPS growth usually means retained earnings are compounding.

ROE · Return on Equity

Net profit as a percentage of shareholders' equity. How hard the company's own capital is working. Consistently high ROE is rare and worth noticing; check whether leverage, not operations, drives it.

ROA · Return on Assets

Net profit as a percentage of total assets. How efficiently the whole asset base earns. Banks and asset-heavy firms live on different ROA scales, so compare within the sector, never across.

Dividend yield · Cash in hand

Annual dividend per share divided by the market price. The cash return you get for holding. A very high yield can signal a falling price or an unsustainable payout; read the history, not one year.

PEG · Growth-adjusted P/E

P/E divided by the earnings growth rate. Tries to answer whether a high P/E is justified by growth. Needs reliable multi-year earnings figures, which is why it is shown here as a concept only.

What this page covers, and what it does not.

Every figure on this page comes from two real sources: the Alpha Lab engine's daily batch (prices, volumes, sector tags, rule-based verdicts) and the companies' own published quarterly filings (EPS, P/E, net profit, NPL, deposits, loans for covered banks). Figures are shown exactly as published, never estimated.

Coverage is partial: quarterly filings are currently compiled for 19 commercial banks, and the table above grows as more published statements are verified. Companies without published figures in our coverage keep the honest "not yet published" label instead of a guess.

No discounted cash flow, no intrinsic value, no price targets. NEPSE disclosure data cannot support honest valuations, and presenting them would be theater, not analysis.

The fine print.

  • All figures come from the daily batch; check the "updated" badge for the batch date. Freshness varies by security.
  • Sector medians are computed from the latest batch over securities with data; thin or suspended listings may be excluded.
  • Securities without a recorded sector appear as Unclassified and get no sector comparison.
  • Headlines are collected from Nepali business media and link to the source; they are not our reporting.
