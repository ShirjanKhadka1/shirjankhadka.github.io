    How does the Distribution Radar work?

    The v2 verdict engine analyses price action, volume patterns, and market regime for every NEPSE-listed equity daily. Distribution Warning flags stocks showing operator distribution footprints. Elevated Risk flags deteriorating technical structure. Bounce Setup flags oversold conditions with reversal potential. All rules are transparent, and every warning is tracked forward for 20 sessions. Educational use only; not investment advice.

    About the distribution radar

    The distribution radar watches the whole market for the footprints left when operators distribute stock: climax tops, fading winners, and pump-like runs that stall. Every listed security is scored daily by one transparent, rule-based engine, and the board above shows the names carrying a flag right now — distribution warnings, elevated risk, and bounce setups — with each flag’s reason, confidence, and sector.

    The board is not a tip sheet. A distribution flag says the chart looks like distribution, not that the stock will fall; a bounce setup says the stock is oversold with reversal potential, not that it will bounce. The discipline is in the tracking: every warning ever issued stays on the ledger for 20 sessions, and the track record above reports exactly how many are still open, provisionally hit, or resolved. Until warnings resolve, no hit rate is shown — a blank rate is honest, never a placeholder.

    The radar rebuilds daily from the last closed session, and the session date is printed above the table. NEPSE publishes no free official real-time feed, so the board works from the daily batch — figures are final only after the close.

    Signals are mechanical outputs of published rules for education. A flag is the engine’s summary of what the chart looks like right now, not a recommendation — past patterns do not guarantee future results, and you should do your own research before any investment decision.

    Distribution radar questions

    What is a Distribution Warning?

    A stock showing operator distribution footprints: pump-like price action with bearish flags such as climax tops and fading winners. The flag names its reason on the row.

    What is Elevated Risk?

    Deteriorating technical structure that has not yet printed a full distribution footprint — a watch flag, not a sell call.

    What is a Bounce Setup?

    An oversold condition with reversal potential. It is the radar’s constructive flag — the other side of the risk board.

    How often is the radar updated?

    The engine rebuilds daily from the last closed session; the session date and ledger update time are printed above the table.

    What does the track record show?

    Every warning ever issued, tracked forward for 20 sessions: how many are still open, provisionally hit, or resolved. Until warnings resolve, no hit rate is shown.

    Is this investment advice?

    No. This is a mechanical, rule-based illustration for education. Markets are uncertain and past patterns do not guarantee future results. Do your own research before any investment decision.
