How does the Warning Radar work?

The v2 verdict engine analyses price action, volume patterns, and market regime for every NEPSE-listed equity daily. Distribution Warning flags stocks showing operator distribution footprints. Elevated Risk flags deteriorating technical structure. Bounce Setup flags oversold conditions with reversal potential. All rules are transparent. Educational use only; not investment advice.

About the NEPSE screener

The NEPSE screener ranks the whole market so you don’t have to open 400 charts. Every listed security is scored by one transparent, rule-based engine on ten factors — trend versus moving averages, RSI, MACD, turnover, 52-week position, divergences, chart patterns, market backdrop and support/resistance — and mapped to a verdict from Strong Buy to Strong Exit. Every factor and its contribution is listed, so nothing is a black box.

Each row carries the numbers a trader actually checks: the latest price and day change, RSI(14), an ATR-based stop-loss and target framing a 1:2 risk-reward, and the engine’s hit rate — how often its past Buy calls on that security reached their target before their stop. The static snapshot above shows the day’s top 10; the full interactive table below ranks every listed security with search, sector and signal filters.

The ranking is rebuilt daily from the last closed session, and the session date is printed above the table. NEPSE publishes no free official real-time API, so the screener works from the daily batch and free community mirrors — figures can lag during market hours and are final only after the close.

Signals are mechanical outputs of published rules for education. A Strong Buy is the engine’s summary of what the chart looks like right now, not a recommendation — past patterns do not guarantee future results, and you should do your own research before any investment decision.

Screener questions

How are the screener signals computed?

Each listed security is scored by a rule-based engine on ten transparent factors: trend versus moving averages, RSI, MACD, turnover, 52-week position, divergences, chart patterns, market backdrop and support/resistance. Scores map to Strong Buy, Buy, Hold, Exit/Reduce or Strong Exit. Stop-loss sits 2× ATR(14) below the last close and the target 4× ATR(14) above it, a 1:2 risk-reward frame.

How often is the ranking updated?

The ranking is rebuilt daily from the last closed session. The date of the data is shown above the table.

What do the RSI extremes and Moves most lists show?

RSI extremes groups the latest batch by RSI(14): oversold below 30, overbought above 70, plus the ten lowest and ten highest readings. Moves most ranks securities by their 20-session average daily range (high minus low) as a share of the latest close. Both are mechanical measurements for education, not recommendations.

Is this investment advice?

No. This is a mechanical, rule-based illustration for education. Markets are uncertain and past patterns do not guarantee future results. Do your own research before any investment decision.

What is the Hit rate column?

For each security with enough history, the engine’s past Buy and Strong Buy calls on equity securities over the last 250 sessions are replayed using only data available at the time. A call counts as a hit when the session’s own target (+4x ATR) was reached before its stop-loss (-2x ATR) within the next 20 sessions. The hit rate is hits divided by decided calls; calls where neither level was touched are excluded. Fewer than 5 decided calls shows as a dash.
