The four factors, in plain language

Earnings yield (1 ÷ P/E)

What the business earns for every rupee of price you pay. Higher means you are paying less for each rupee of earnings.

Limit: earnings can be one-off or cyclical — a high yield can be a trap.

Price-to-book (P/BV)

Price versus the accounting value of net assets per share. Below 1 means you pay less than book value.

Limit: book value may overstate assets that are hard to sell, like bad loans.

ROE (TTM)

Return on equity: how much profit each rupee of shareholder capital generated over the last twelve months.

Limit: high ROE from heavy leverage is fragile, not strong.

P/E (TTM)

How many years of current earnings the price implies. Lower usually means cheaper — within the same sector.

Limit: meaningless for loss-making companies; we exclude those.
