The rules (exactly as coded)

  1. Setup: price below 50-day SMA for at least 20 of the last 30 sessions (was weak)
  2. Trigger: 20-day SMA crosses above 50-day SMA with volume over 1.5 × 20-day average
  3. Filter: 20-day avg turnover above Rs 10 lakh
  4. Stop: 2 × ATR(14) below entry. Trail: 20-day SMA cross back below 50-day SMA, or 3 closes under the 50-day SMA
  5. Targets: T1 = entry + 1.5 × risk, T2 = entry + 3 × risk (reference levels — shown for position management, not traded in the backtest)

Plain words: what these terms mean

ATR
Average True Range — how much the price typically moves in a day. The system uses it to size stops: a bigger ATR means a wider stop.
RSI
Relative Strength Index (0–100) — measures how fast the price has been rising vs falling. Above 70 is often called overbought, below 30 oversold.
SMA
Simple Moving Average — the average closing price over N days. Price above its SMA is in an uptrend on that timeframe.
Breakout
The price closing above a recent high — the momentum system's entry trigger.
Risk per trade
How much of the portfolio is risked if the stop is hit. These backtests risk a fixed rupee amount per position.
T1 / T2 targets
Reference price levels shown for context only — the backtest never exits at them; exits follow the coded stop/trail/mean rules.
Win rate
Share of closed trades that made money. Below 50% can still profit if winners are bigger than losers.
Profit factor
Total gains divided by total losses. Above 1.0 means the system made money overall.
Max drawdown
The worst peak-to-trough fall of the portfolio — the pain you'd have sat through.
Alpha vs NEPSE
How much the system's return beat (or lagged) simply holding the NEPSE index.
Sharpe / Sortino
Return per unit of risk taken. Higher is better; negative means the return didn't justify the bumps.
Turnover filter
Stocks must average at least Rs 10 lakh daily turnover to qualify — so signals only fire on names you could actually trade.
