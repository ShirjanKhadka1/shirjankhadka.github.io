Broker Smart Money

"Smart money" is usually a label asserted about the biggest brokers. Here it is measured: every broker is scored on its matured accumulation events — the stocks it net-bought over trailing 30-day windows, and what those stocks did over the next 10 sessions against the average stock. The brokers whose buying actually beat the market form a top-decile skilled cohort, chosen only on evidence from before the current window opened — and this board follows where that cohort's money is accumulating now. The full leaderboard is published, not teased, and so is the uncomfortable part: a split-half check of whether the skill ranking persisted inside its own era (below — it largely did not).

    How this board is built

    The definition. "Smart money" on this page means brokers whose past net accumulation measurably beat the market — not the biggest brokers, and not a label anyone asserts. The equivalent commercial board sells the same idea with its rows locked and its parameters undisclosed; every parameter below is ours, published, and every row here is free.

    Skill. At weekly snapshots (every 5 sessions), an accumulation event is a broker that net-bought a stock over the trailing 30 calendar days. The event's outcome is that stock's return over the next 10 sessions (final, corporate-action-adjusted closes) minus the equal-weight mean return of every measurable stock over the same span — "the market" here is the average stock, because the official index history has gaps on archive sessions. Events whose forward move exceeds ±15% are dropped (corporate-action resets the adjusted series does not carry) and counted in the payload. A broker's skill is the net-amount-weighted mean outcome of its events, in percentage points; its hit rate is the share of events that beat the average stock. Brokers are rated at 5+ events and decile-eligible at 30+ events scored in at least half the snapshots; the cohort is the top tenth of the eligible brokers.

    No lookahead. The board's cohort and quality gauges use only snapshots whose 10-session outcome window ended before the board's 30-day window opened. The leaderboard's full-era skill uses every matured snapshot — including outcomes inside the board window — and is published as a record, never as an input to the board.

    The board. For each stock-like name (debentures and mutual funds stay on the Zero-Sum board), the cohort's signed net flow over the window is the skilled net; intensity is that flow as a share of the stock's turnover. Broker quality is the net-amount-weighted skill of the rated brokers accumulating the stock. Conviction is the mean of the two board-wide percentile ranks (skilled net, quality), tiered Very High ≥ 80, High ≥ 60, Moderate ≥ 40, Low below — names the cohort is net selling or not touching sit at Watch / None and are listed on the distribution side instead. Newly started means the cohort's net was not positive in the previous 30 days; price-confirmed means the stock's 20-session momentum is positive alongside the accumulation.

    The honest limits. The floorsheet archive starts 2026-07-15, so the whole skill era is about three months, in one market regime, from overlapping windows — event counts look large because the same positions recur across snapshots. The split-half check above is the number that disciplines this page: picked on the era's first half, the cohort's edge in the second half was close to zero. Read the board as a map of where measured-skilled flow currently sits — description, not prediction. Broker data is transaction flow — what moved through each broker — never a record of what any investor holds. The 2026-10-07 session is held as broker totals only (no per-stock rows), so windows spanning it rest on the sessions the archive holds.
