Reading strategy

How to read this screener

A five-step routine the engine itself follows — use it to audit every signal before you trust it.

  1. 1

    Start with the market verdict

    The score above blends four real factors — signal breadth, score momentum, RSI posture and participation. Trade with the wind: in a Risk-off tape, even Strong Buys deserve smaller size.

  2. 2

    Check the trend gate

    No Buy is issued while a price sits below its 200-day average. If the setup column is empty and the signal is Hold, the engine sees no edge — believe it.

  3. 3

    Read RSI in context

    Below 30 is oversold fuel, above 70 is overbought caution — but an oversold stock in a downtrend is a falling knife, not a bargain. Pair RSI with the trend.

  4. 4

    Respect the stop-loss

    Every row carries an ATR-based stop (2× ATR) and target (4× ATR) — a 1:2 risk-reward frame. If the stop feels too far for your capital, the position is too big.

  5. 5

    Verify the hit rate

    The Hit rate column replays the engine's past Buy calls on that security. High hit rates in the old regime can collapse in the new one — the aggregate track record, with its window dates and sample size, is published in the methodology note below. Recency matters.

  6. 6

    Confirm with volume

    A breakout on thin volume is a rumor; on heavy volume it is a vote. The Moves most tab shows where real energy is going each session.

A gentle reminder: no model — this one included — is right 100% of the time. The engine is a mechanical checklist, not a crystal ball: it summarizes what the chart looks like right now. Markets are uncertain, regimes change, and past patterns do not guarantee future results. Use these signals as a starting point for your own research, never as instructions.

About the NEPSE screener

The NEPSE screener ranks the whole market so you don’t have to open 400 charts. Every listed security is scored by one transparent, rule-based engine on ten factors — trend versus moving averages, RSI, MACD, turnover, 52-week position, divergences, chart patterns, market backdrop and support/resistance — and mapped to a verdict from Strong Buy to Strong Exit. Every factor and its contribution is listed, so nothing is a black box.

Each row carries the numbers a trader actually checks: the latest price and day change, RSI(14), an ATR-based stop-loss and target framing a 1:2 risk-reward, and the engine’s hit rate — how often its past Buy calls on that security reached their target before their stop. The RSI tables above show the day’s extremes; the full interactive table below ranks all securities with search, sector and signal filters.

The ranking is rebuilt daily from the last closed session, and the session date is printed above the table. NEPSE publishes no free official real-time API, so the screener works from the daily batch and free community mirrors — figures can lag during market hours and are final only after the close.

Signals are mechanical outputs of published rules for education. A Strong Buy is the engine’s ranked setup score — a summary of what the chart looks like right now, not a recommendation — for research and education only — past patterns do not guarantee future results, and you should do your own research before any investment decision.

Screener questions

How are the screener signals computed?

Each listed security is scored by a rule-based engine on ten transparent factors: trend versus moving averages, RSI, MACD, turnover, 52-week position, divergences, chart patterns, market backdrop and support/resistance. Scores map to Strong Buy, Buy, Hold, Exit/Reduce or Strong Exit. Stop-loss sits 2× ATR(14) below the last close and the target 4× ATR(14) above it, a 1:2 risk-reward frame.

How often is the ranking updated?

The ranking is rebuilt daily from the last closed session. The date of the data is shown above the table.

What do the RSI extremes and Moves most tables show?

RSI extremes groups the latest batch by RSI(14): oversold below 30, overbought above 70, plus the ten lowest and ten highest readings. Moves most ranks securities by their 20-session average daily range (high minus low) as a share of the latest close. Both are mechanical measurements for education, not recommendations.

Is this investment advice?

No. This is a mechanical, rule-based illustration for education. Markets are uncertain and past patterns do not guarantee future results. Do your own research before any investment decision. Buy and Strong Buy labels are ranked setup scores, not recommendations.

What is the Hit rate column?

For each security with enough history, the engine’s past Buy and Strong Buy calls on equity securities over the last 250 sessions are replayed using only data available at the time. A call counts as a hit when the session’s own target (+4x ATR) was reached before its stop-loss (-2x ATR) within the next 20 sessions. The hit rate is hits divided by decided calls; calls where neither level was touched are excluded. Fewer than 5 decided calls shows as a dash.
