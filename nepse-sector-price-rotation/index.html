Does leadership stick?

Measured on weekly snapshots of the trailing 3 years of these same derived indices — how often the sector ranked #1 on a window was still in the top 3 four weeks later, and how similar boards four weeks apart typically are (rank correlation, −1 to +1)

The pattern is the point: at 7 and 30 days, leadership is close to a coin flip a month later — short-window “rotation” is mostly noise. At 180 and 365 days, the leader almost always stays near the top. These are descriptions of the past three years, not forecasts.

How this board is built

The sectors. Each sector is a derived index built by Nepse Decode: the equal-weighted chained daily returns of the sector's listed companies, computed from our own OHLC archive (the same series behind the Seasonality, Market Regime and Sector Rotation pages). Sector membership is today's map applied historically, so delisted names are absent — a survivorship caveat we state rather than hide. Our taxonomy has 11 sectors: Trading companies are folded into Others, and mutual funds are an instrument type rather than a sector index.

The window. Timeframes are calendar days. For a window of N days, the base is the last close on or before the date N days before the latest session, and the end is the latest session's close. The sector's return is its derived index's change between those two points; ranks order the sectors by that return. Rank Δ 1W compares today's rank with the same window's board computed one week earlier.

Breadth and movers. Each constituent stock's own close-to-close return is computed over the identical window. Breadth counts how many rose and how many fell (an unchanged stock counts in neither); Priced counts the stocks with a close on or before both window ends, out of the sector's membership. The movers are simply the three largest gains and the three largest falls among those stocks — by their own returns, not weighted contributions.

Persistence. The “Does leadership stick?” figures replay this exact board on weekly snapshots across the trailing three years of our derived indices: for each timeframe, the share of weeks whose #1 sector was still top-3 four weeks later, and the mean Spearman rank correlation between boards four weeks apart. They are measured history, published so the board's short windows are read with the scepticism their own record earns.

Reading it honestly. Price rotation shows where returns have concentrated, in absolute terms. It is a map of the recent past — not a signal, not a valuation, and not a promise about which sector leads next; at short windows the persistence numbers above say leadership usually does not carry over.

The stocks panel. The board shows only the three largest movers per sector; the stocks panel lists every priced constituent of the selected sector with its own return over the selected window, from the same computation. Counts reconcile to the board's Priced column; a dash means the stock has no close on or before both window ends.

Forward tracking. Separately from the 3-year persistence statistics, each published board's per-window leader and top-3 are written to a forward ledger, and 20 sessions later the leader's forward composite return and whether it is still top-3 are resolved from our own series. No entry is ever backfilled; the launch board is the live 2026-10-16 board, and its windows are still open at launch.

Session grid and refreshing. The session grid is capped at our archive's latest session, so a stray index-history row on a holiday (the 2026-10-19 stub) can never become the "one week ago" board or a weekly snapshot; dropped rows are listed in the payload's benchmark_guard. The page re-checks the data on the market-aware schedule — every 5 minutes during market hours, 30 minutes otherwise, 2 hours on weekends — and refreshes silently when a new board lands.
