Book closure: the date that decides who gets the dividend

Unilever Nepal has proposed a 1,540% cash dividend, Sarbottam Cement 21% and Sonapur Minerals 5.263% — but the money goes only to shareholders on the register when it freezes. The deadline is the trading day before book close: 24 November for UNL, 25 November for SONA, 3 December for SARBTM.

A company declares a 21% dividend. You own its shares. You expect the money. Then you learn the dividend went to someone else — because you bought one day too late.

That is what book closure does. It is a single date, announced in advance, on which a company freezes its list of shareholders. Whoever is on the list that day gets the dividend, the bonus shares, the right shares, the AGM vote. Whoever is not on it gets nothing from that corporate action — even if they bought the shares the same week.

The rule that decides it is exactly one day wide, and it catches investors every dividend season. This article works through the rule and the three live book closures on NEPSE right now — Unilever Nepal, Sonapur Minerals and Sarbottam Cement — so the deadline is a date on a calendar, not a surprise.

Signal summary

  • Book closure is the date a company freezes its share register. Whoever is on the register that day is entitled to the dividend, bonus shares, right shares or AGM vote. It is not a trading halt — the shares keep trading through it.
  • The deadline is the trading day before book close. Buy by that day and you are eligible; buy on book-close day itself and you miss it. All three live notices say so in their own wording.
  • The three live deadlines this season: UNL 24 November, SONA 25 November, SARBTM 3 December 2026. UNL proposes 1,540% cash, SONA 5.263% cash, SARBTM 21% (6% bonus + 15% cash) — all for FY 2082/83, all still board-proposed and awaiting their annual general meetings.
  • Settlement (T+2) and eligibility run on different clocks. Shares bought the day before book close reach your demat account after the register freezes — but the notices fix eligibility by the trade date, so you are still eligible.
  • Selling before book close hands the dividend to the buyer. Eligibility sits with whoever is on the register, not with whoever held the shares longest.

01. What book closure actually is

A listed company keeps a register of its shareholders — the record of who owns how many shares. When it declares a dividend, it has to know who to pay. So it announces a book closure date and, on that date, it stops updating the register. The names on the register at that moment are the shareholders entitled to the corporate action.

The closure usually lasts one day. Sometimes longer: Unilever Nepal’s current book closure runs fifteen days, Mangsir 9–23, 2083 (25 November–9 December 2026). Either way, the logic is the same — the register freezes, and the freeze fixes who gets paid.

Book closure is not a trading halt. The shares keep trading through it. What stops is the updating of the entitlement list, not the market.

One honest gap: no page on NEPSE’s official site defines book closure, so the definition in this article is a plain-language restatement of what the three official notices below use the term to mean. Nothing here is quoted as a NEPSE definition.

02. The one rule: buy by the trading day before

The rule is simple, and it is written into the companies’ own notices to NEPSE — not into a broker’s blog or a portal’s summary:

Buy by the trading day before book close. Buy on book-close day itself, and you miss it.

Three live notices say it three different ways:

  • Sonapur Minerals (SONA), for its proposed 5.263% cash dividend: book closure Mangsir 10, 2083 (26 November 2026), and “shareholders on record from trading up to Mangsir 9, 2083 are eligible” — buy by 25 November.
  • Sarbottam Cement (SARBTM), for its proposed 21% dividend: book closure Mangsir 18, 2083 (4 December 2026), and “holders who bought by trading up to Mangsir 17, 2083 (3 December 2026) and had shares transferred into their names are eligible” — buy by 3 December.
  • Unilever Nepal (UNL), for its proposed 1,540% cash dividend: book closure Mangsir 9–23, 2083 (25 November–9 December 2026), and “shareholders on record through Mangsir 8, 2083 eligible” — buy by 24 November.

Three companies, three notices, one rule. The wording differs; the deadline does not.

04. Why the day before works, even though settlement takes two days

This is the part that confuses people. NEPSE trades settle on T+2 — shares bought today reach your demat account two working days later. So if you buy SONA on 25 November, the shares land in your demat on 27 November, a day after the book closes on 26 November. How can you still be eligible?

Because the notices fix eligibility by the trade date, not by the settlement date. The entitlement follows the trade: the shares you bought on 25 November carry the dividend with them, even though they arrive in your demat after the register freezes. Settlement is the clock for the shares reaching you; the trade date is the clock for the dividend reaching you. They are two different clocks, and the notices are explicit about which one counts.

The same logic runs in reverse, and it is worth stating plainly: if you sell before book close, the buyer gets the dividend, not you. Eligibility sits with whoever is on the register — and the register is fixed by who bought by the day before, not by who held the shares longest.

05. What book closure does not do

Book closure does not move the price by itself. After a bonus issue, the price adjusts — a 10% bonus turns a Rs 100 share into roughly Rs 90.91, because the same company is now divided into more shares — but that adjustment comes from the bonus, not from the closure. A cash dividend does not mechanically change the price at all. The closure only decides who receives what has been declared.

It also does not protect a proposed dividend. All three dividends in the table above are board proposals. An annual general meeting can approve, reduce or reject them. Being on the register buys you a seat at the distribution — it does not guarantee the distribution happens.

And it does not forgive a missed deadline by a day. There is no grace period, no “I bought on the morning of book-close day” exception. The register freezes when it freezes.

06. What to take from it

  1. Before buying any share for its dividend, find the book closure date first — it is in the company’s notice to NEPSE, and it is the only date that decides eligibility.
  2. The deadline is the trading day before book close, not the book-close day itself. One day late is too late.
  3. The three live deadlines this season: UNL 24 November, SONA 25 November, SARBTM 3 December 2026 — for dividends that are proposed, not yet approved.
  4. Settlement (T+2) and eligibility run on different clocks. The notices decide eligibility by trade date.
  5. Selling before book close hands the dividend to the buyer. The register, not the price and not how long you held, decides who gets paid.

Book closure is the least dramatic date in the dividend calendar and the one that costs investors the most when they miss it. It is printed in every dividend notice, in plain words, weeks in advance. The only mistake is not reading it.

Frequently asked questions

What is book closure?

The date on which a company freezes its share register to fix the list of shareholders entitled to a dividend, bonus shares, right shares or AGM vote. Whoever is on the register that day gets the corporate action.

What is the last day to buy shares to get the dividend?

The trading day before the book closure date. SONA’s book closes 26 November 2026, so the last day to buy is 25 November; SARBTM’s closes 4 December, so the last day is 3 December; UNL’s closes 25 November, so the last day is 24 November.

I bought on book-close day itself. Do I get the dividend?

No. The notices state eligibility from trading up to the day before book close. Buying on the day itself is too late.

My shares settle T+2, after book close. Am I still eligible?

Yes, if you bought by the trading day before. The notices fix eligibility by the trade date, not by the day the shares reach your demat account.

If I sell before book close, do I keep the dividend?

No. Eligibility sits with whoever is on the register on book-close day. Selling before then hands the entitlement to the buyer.

Does book closure change the share price?

Not by itself. A bonus issue adjusts the price because the same company is divided into more shares; a cash dividend has no mechanical price effect. The closure only decides who receives what was declared.

Are the UNL, SONA and SARBTM dividends final?

No. All three are board-proposed for FY 2082/83 and still need their annual general meetings’ approval. The book closure dates are fixed; the dividends are not.
