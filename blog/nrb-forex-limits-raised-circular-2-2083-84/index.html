NRB raised forex limits two to five times in a single circular. Whether reserves can keep up is the open question

Circular No. 2/2083-84 amends the Unified Circular 2082: the USD 15,000 annual exchange facility doubles to USD 30,000, airline advance payments jump fivefold to USD 500,000, and IT plus energy join the privileged foreign-loan guarantee list. The numbers, the mechanics, and the honest limits.

By Shirjan Khadka · Founder, Nepse Decode, Kathmandu · October 7, 2026 · 5 min read
Facebook X LinkedIn WhatsApp Viber

The Nepal Rastra Bank forex circular 2083 now in force is the single biggest loosening of foreign-exchange rules Nepal's central bank has issued in one document. The Foreign Exchange Management Department issued Circular No. 2/2083-84, dated October 5, 2026 and signed by Executive Director Dr. Dilliram Pokhrel, amending the Unified Circular 2082 that governs every foreign-exchange transaction routed through Nepal's formal channels. The headline change is the one ordinary Nepalis will feel first: the annual exchange facility accessible through banks and financial institutions doubles from USD 15,000 to USD 30,000. This is a genuine policy move, and it deserves a careful reading before anyone prices it in.

The retail caps all move in the same direction. The per-transaction ceiling for service imports and the service-import advance both rise from USD 15,000 to USD 30,000, and payments to Indian entities per transaction climb from INR 40 lakh to INR 60 lakh. On the card side, the mandatory-deposit threshold for card-based foreign exchange doubles from USD 5,000 to USD 10,000, and the annual spending limit on cards issued to service exporters rises from USD 5,000 to USD 10,000. Every one of these is a permission, not a mandate: the circular lets more dollars out, it does not push them out.

The commercial side is where the jumps are largest. Nepali airlines may now make advance payments of up to USD 500,000 for aircraft, engines and spare parts, up from USD 100,000, a fivefold increase that removes a long-standing bottleneck for a fleet that is overwhelmingly leased and serviced abroad. Nepali telecom operators' annual payment ceiling for satellite services rises the same way, from USD 100,000 to USD 500,000. And in the change with the widest economic reach, A-class banks may now issue foreign-exchange guarantees and standby letters of credit for foreign loans to the information-technology industry and the energy sector, which join commercial agriculture, manufacturing, infrastructure and tourism on the privileged list. A new customs classification, HS code 2710.19.14 for rubber processing oil, is added to the tradable list.

The liberalization has a counterweight, and it is deliberate. The same circular tightens the shipment and draft/TT payment rules: importers must now remit a minimum 10 percent advance before shipment, and the full payment must be completed before the goods are released from customs. Read together, the two halves make NRB's position clear. The central bank is willing to let more foreign exchange flow out through legitimate channels, but it wants the payment plumbing verified before the money moves. Permission widens; supervision tightens.

The attribution is worth recording because this is where rumors usually start. The circular carries the title “२/२०८३–८४ः एकीकृत परिपत्र–२०८२ मा संशोधन”, is signed by Executive Director Dr. Dilliram Pokhrel, and is dated 2083/06/19, which is October 5, 2026. It sits on NRB's official Foreign Exchange Management circulars archive; there is no English summary on the site. Nothing in the document compels any bank, airline or telecom to spend a single extra dollar. It permits and supervises. Whether the raised caps translate into actual outflows depends on demand that was already pressing against the old ceilings, and on how the balance of payments absorbs it in the coming quarters.

For investors the honest meter is the quarterly balance-of-payments data. When the service-import and travel outflow lines start reflecting the new ceilings, they will show whether this circular moved money or merely moved a rule. Until then, the policy is in force and the flows are not yet visible. Watch the data, not the commentary.
