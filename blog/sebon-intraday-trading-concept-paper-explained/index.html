SEBON Proposes Intraday Trading for NEPSE: Buy in the Morning, Sell by Afternoon, Short Selling Waits

The regulator published its concept paper on September 24 and asked investors for feedback. Day trading is not live. Here is what the paper actually proposes, and the long road before it.

By Shirjan Khadka Financial Analyst, Kathmandu
Published September 28, 2026 · 08:30 NPT Updated September 28, 2026 · 08:30 NPT 6 min read
Share Facebook X LinkedIn

At a glance

  • What happened: On September 24, 2026, SEBON published the "Concept Paper on the Implementation of Intraday Trading of Securities in Nepal, 2083" and invited public feedback.
  • What it proposes: Start with "Buy First, Sell Later" intraday trades on a limited set of securities, then expand after a pilot phase.
  • What it does not do: Short selling ("Sell First, Buy Later") waits until securities lending and borrowing infrastructure is built. No launch date. Nothing changes for traders today.
  • What to watch: The feedback window, then regulations, broker procedures, margin systems, and market surveillance capacity.

Picture this. It is 11 in the morning. A stock you hold is up 6% on heavy volume, and everything in you says the rally will fade by afternoon. You want to sell, right now, today, and lock it in. On NEPSE, you cannot. Same-day round trips simply do not exist in the current system. So you watch. By 3 o'clock, half the gain is gone, and you are left doing the math on what could have been.

That frustration is exactly what SEBON is talking about. On September 24, 2026, the Securities Board of Nepal published a formal concept paper on intraday trading (अन्तरदिवसीय कारोबार) and asked investors for feedback. The rumor version of this story will travel faster than the real one, so here is the honest version first: intraday trading is not live, no launch date has been announced, and nothing about your trading changes today. What exists is a proposal. A serious one, worth reading properly.

What did SEBON actually publish?

The document is the "Concept Paper on the Implementation of Intraday Trading of Securities in Nepal, 2083," published with an executive summary on SEBON's official website. It flows from the Ministry of Finance's "Capital Market Strengthening and Revival Action Plan, 2083," issued on 2083/05/29, which put both margin lending and intraday trading on this fiscal year's reform agenda. SEBON has listed specific topics on which it is inviting suggestions, which means this is a genuine consultation, not a done deal.

SEBON states three goals: deeper market liquidity, more effective price discovery, and modern capital market infrastructure. And it puts investor protection first among its guiding principles, ahead of risk-based regulation, phased implementation, and technology-driven real-time monitoring. That ordering is deliberate, and it shapes everything below.

How would it work at first? Buy in the morning, out by afternoon

Here is the concrete picture. You buy 100 units of a stock at 10:30. At 2:15 it is up 3%, and you sell all 100. You go home holding no shares and carrying no overnight risk. That is "Buy First, Sell Later," and it is the only flavor SEBON wants to allow at the start.

Two brakes are built in. First, only a limited set of securities would qualify at the beginning, with the scope expanding only after the pilot phase and a fresh risk assessment. Second, the rollout must fit Nepal's actual market structure and institutional readiness. This is a regulator saying, in effect: we will move slowly on purpose.

Why does short selling have to wait?

Short selling means selling shares you do not own, betting the price will fall so you can buy them back cheaper. For that to work without blowing up the settlement system, someone has to lend you the shares, with collateral rules, margin systems, and a clearing house standing behind the whole chain. Nepal does not have that plumbing yet.

So SEBON's sequencing is "Sell First, Buy Later" only later, after securities lending and borrowing, and covered short selling, are properly built. It is the right order. A market that allowed naked shorting before the lending infrastructure existed would be asking for settlement failures.

What has to happen before it goes live? The to-do list is long

Before intraday trading goes fully live, SEBON says the market needs: the legal and regulatory framework, criteria for which securities and which investors qualify, standard operating procedures for brokers, formal risk disclosure norms, margin and risk management systems, and surveillance capacity to watch it all in real time.

A coordination committee is already named, with SEBON, the Nepal Stock Exchange, CDS and Clearing Ltd., and the Stock Brokers' Association of Nepal at one table. When four institutions have to move together, the file is moving, but four institutions' paperwork is not a launch date. Read that list as a timeline, not a formality. This is why nobody should expect intraday trading next month.

Who benefits, and who should be careful?

The honest answer has two sides. Active investors would get real flexibility: the ability to exit a position the same day instead of carrying overnight risk, and to act on news while it is fresh instead of watching from the sidelines. And there is a quiet win most people miss. Even if you never day-trade, more liquidity makes your trades cheaper and fairer. Tighter spreads, faster fills, prices that actually reflect supply and demand.

The other side is just as real. Every market that has intraday trading teaches the same lesson: speed plus overconfidence is the fastest way beginners lose money. The costs are concrete too. Every round trip pays your broker twice, and small gains die in fees before they ever reach your pocket. Intraday trading rewards discipline and punishes impulse, and most retail accounts run on impulse.

SEBON's design, buy-first-only, limited securities, eligibility criteria for investors, reads like a regulator that knows all of this. The right response is not excitement or fear. It is to read the paper, send feedback while the window is open, and keep trading exactly as before until there is an actual regulation with an actual date.

This article is research and education, not investment advice. It summarizes SEBON's official concept paper published on September 24, 2026; for the full text and the feedback topics, see the original notice on sebon.gov.np. Nothing described here is yet in force.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is intraday trading live on NEPSE now?
No. On September 24, 2026, SEBON published only a concept paper on intraday trading and invited public feedback. No regulation has been issued, no launch date announced, and nothing changes in how you trade today.
What does SEBON's intraday trading concept paper propose?
A phased rollout: start with "Buy First, Sell Later" intraday trades on a limited set of eligible securities, then expand scope after a pilot phase. "Sell First, Buy Later" (short selling) would come only after securities lending and borrowing plus covered short selling infrastructure is sufficiently developed.
What has to happen before intraday trading can start on NEPSE?
According to SEBON, the preconditions include the legal and regulatory framework, criteria for eligible securities and eligible investors, broker standard operating procedures, risk disclosure norms, margin and risk management systems, and market surveillance capacity.
Why does SEBON want intraday trading?
SEBON cites three goals: deeper market liquidity, more effective price discovery, and modern capital market infrastructure. The proposal flows from the Ministry of Finance's Capital Market Strengthening and Revival Action Plan, 2083, with investor protection stated as the first priority.
Can investors give feedback on the intraday proposal?
Yes. SEBON published the concept paper with an executive summary and listed the topics on which it wants suggestions. Investors can submit feedback through the channel SEBON indicated on its official notice page at sebon.gov.np.
