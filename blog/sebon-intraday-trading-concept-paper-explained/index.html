SEBON Floats Intraday Trading for NEPSE: What the Concept Paper Actually Says

By Shirjan Khadka September 28, 2026 5 min read

Photo: Bishaldev100 / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0)

On September 24, 2026, the Securities Board of Nepal did something it has never done before: it published a formal concept paper on intraday trading (अन्तरदिवसीय कारोबार) for the Nepali stock market and asked investors for feedback. If you trade on NEPSE, this is worth understanding properly, because the rumor version of this story will travel faster than the real one. So here is the honest version first: intraday trading is not live, no launch date has been announced, and nothing about your trading changes today. What exists is a proposal, and a good one to read carefully.

What SEBON Actually Published

The document is titled the "Concept Paper on the Implementation of Intraday Trading of Securities in Nepal, 2083," published with an executive summary on SEBON's official website. It flows from the Ministry of Finance's "Capital Market Strengthening and Revival Action Plan, 2083," issued on 2083/05/29, which put both margin lending and intraday trading on this fiscal year's reform agenda. SEBON has listed specific topics on which it is inviting suggestions, which means this is a genuine consultation, not a done deal.

SEBON states three goals for the reform: deeper market liquidity, more effective price discovery, and modern capital market infrastructure. And it puts investor protection first in its list of guiding principles, alongside risk-based regulation, phased implementation, technology-driven real-time trading and monitoring, and transparent market operation. That ordering matters, as you will see below.

How It Would Work at First: Buy First, Sell Later

The paper proposes a deliberately cautious start. In the initial phase, only "Buy First, Sell Later" intraday trades would be allowed: you buy a stock and sell it the same day. What you could not do at first is the reverse, "Sell First, Buy Later", which is short selling. That comes only after the infrastructure for securities lending and borrowing, and for covered short selling, is sufficiently developed.

Two more brakes are built in. First, only a limited set of securities would qualify for intraday trading at the start, with the scope expanding only after the pilot phase's experience and a fresh risk assessment. Second, the rollout must account for Nepal's current market structure, institutional capacity, and risk management readiness. This is a regulator saying, in effect: we will move slowly on purpose.

What Has to Happen Before Launch

SEBON lists the preconditions plainly, and the list is long enough to tell you this is not imminent. Before intraday trading goes fully live, the market needs: the legal and regulatory framework, criteria for which securities and which investors qualify, standard operating procedures for brokers, formal risk disclosure norms, margin and risk management systems, and surveillance capacity to watch it all in real time.

A coordination and facilitation committee is already named for the job, with representatives from SEBON, the Nepal Stock Exchange, CDS and Clearing Ltd., and the Stock Brokers' Association of Nepal. When the regulator, the exchange, the clearing house, and the brokers are all at one table, the file is moving, but paperwork at four institutions is not a launch date.

What This Means for Ordinary Investors

Start with what does not change: your orders, your settlement cycle, and your broker relationship are exactly as they were last week. Now the honest assessment of what could change, if this is done well. Intraday trading gives active investors flexibility they do not have today, the ability to exit a position the same day instead of carrying overnight risk. Done right, with tight surveillance and real risk disclosure, it adds liquidity, and liquidity is what makes every other investor's trades cheaper and fairer.

The caution is equally real. Intraday trading is one of the fastest ways inexperienced investors lose money in every market that has it, because leverage, speed, and overconfidence compound. SEBON's phased design, buy-first-only at the start, limited securities, eligibility criteria for investors, reads like a regulator that knows this. The right response from investors is not excitement or fear. It is to read the paper, send feedback while the window is open, and keep trading exactly as before until there is an actual regulation with an actual date.

This article is research and education, not investment advice. It summarizes SEBON's official concept paper published on September 24, 2026; for the full text and the feedback topics, see the original notice on sebon.gov.np. Nothing described here is yet in force.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is intraday trading live on NEPSE now?
No. On September 24, 2026, SEBON published only a concept paper on intraday trading and invited public feedback. No regulation has been issued, no launch date announced, and nothing changes in how you trade today.
What does SEBON's intraday trading concept paper propose?
A phased rollout: start with "Buy First, Sell Later" intraday trades on a limited set of eligible securities, then expand scope after a pilot phase. "Sell First, Buy Later" (short selling) would come only after securities lending and borrowing plus covered short selling infrastructure is sufficiently developed.
What has to happen before intraday trading can start on NEPSE?
According to SEBON, the preconditions include the legal and regulatory framework, criteria for eligible securities and eligible investors, broker standard operating procedures, risk disclosure norms, margin and risk management systems, and market surveillance capacity.
Why does SEBON want intraday trading?
SEBON cites three goals: deeper market liquidity, more effective price discovery, and modern capital market infrastructure. The proposal flows from the Ministry of Finance's Capital Market Strengthening and Revival Action Plan, 2083, with investor protection stated as the first priority.
Can investors give feedback on the intraday proposal?
Yes. SEBON published the concept paper with an executive summary and listed the topics on which it wants suggestions. Investors can submit feedback through the channel SEBON indicated on its official notice page at sebon.gov.np.
