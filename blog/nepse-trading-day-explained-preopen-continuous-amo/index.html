When does NEPSE actually trade? The four sessions of a trading day

Eleven o’clock is when trading starts. The trading day starts half an hour earlier — and for investors who place orders at night, it never really ends. A NEPSE trading day has four sessions, and each one does a different job. Confusing them is how investors end up wondering why an order placed at 10:40 has not traded, or why an order placed at 8 PM has not moved by morning. Here is the day, session by session.

Ask a new investor when the Nepali stock market opens and you will hear “11 o’clock”. That is the right answer to the wrong question. Eleven is when trading starts. The trading day starts half an hour earlier — and for investors who place orders at night, it never really ends.

A NEPSE trading day has four sessions, and each one does a different job. Two of them take orders without trading a single share. One of them sets the opening price before the market opens. Only one of them is trading at all. This article walks the day in order, with the times as they stand in November 2026 — after the April 2026 change to the trading week and the August 2026 arrival of after-market orders — and says plainly what each session can and cannot do for your order.

Signal summary

  • NEPSE trades Monday to Friday, 11:00 to 15:00. Saturday and Sunday are market holidays, as are Nepali public holidays. The week changed in April 2026, from the old Sunday-to-Thursday schedule; any guide that still lists Sunday to Thursday is out of date.
  • Pre-open runs 10:30 to 11:00, and nothing trades in it. Orders are taken from 10:30 to 10:45 — place, modify, cancel, none executes — each within 5% of the previous close. The queued orders are then matched from 10:45 to 11:00 to set one opening price for each share.
  • Continuous trading, 11:00 to 15:00, is the only session where orders match live. Four hours, on price-time priority: the best price first, and at the same price, the earliest order first. The NEPSE index, the sector indices and the turnover figures update tick by tick inside this window and freeze the moment it closes.
  • There is no after-hours trading on NEPSE. After Market Orders queue between 17:00 and 10:00 — a 17-hour window, more than four times as long as the four-hour continuous session — and are sent to the market when the next session opens. They do not execute overnight and they do not move the price while the market is closed.
  • Pre-open is one-eighth of the session and does the job of setting the start. Thirty minutes of pre-open against 240 minutes of continuous trading: the opening price is the market’s first verdict, computed from the queue before a single live trade happens.

01. First, the week

NEPSE trades Monday to Friday. Saturday and Sunday are market holidays, as are Nepali public holidays announced by NEPSE notice. This is the schedule since April 2026, when the exchange shifted from the old Sunday-to-Thursday week to Monday-to-Friday, aligning with the government’s two-day weekend. Any guide that still lists Sunday to Thursday is out of date.

The week matters to the day because the sessions below assume a trading day. An order queued on a Friday evening waits through the weekend: the after-market window runs overnight, but the next session it can be sent to is Monday morning’s pre-open, not Saturday’s — there is no Saturday session to send it to. The same applies to public holidays: NEPSE publishes the official annual holiday calendar, and a queued order simply waits until the next trading day.

02. 10:30–10:45 — Pre-open: orders in, nothing trades

The first session is pre-open, and its first fifteen minutes are for queuing, not trading. Between 10:30 and 10:45 you can place an order, change it, or cancel it — but not one order executes. The system collects them.

There is one guardrail: a pre-open order must sit within 5% of the previous day’s close. That is a narrower band than the 15% daily price band that applies once trading is live, and it exists for the same reason — to keep the opening price from jumping on a single wild order. An order outside the 5% band is not accepted into the pre-open queue at all.

This is the session that confuses new investors most, because the market-status pill on a trading app already reads “Pre-Open” at 10:30 and the order screen is live. The screen being live is not the market being open. At 10:40 your order is a name on a list, not a trade — and it will stay a name on a list until the matching window below.

03. 10:45–11:00 — Pre-open: the queue sets the opening price

In the second half of pre-open, the queued orders are matched against each other to set one opening price for each share. The TMS lists this window as its own session — PREOPEN, 10:45:00 to 10:59:59 — and it is the window Nepse Decode’s own market-status data uses. The two framings of pre-open are not a contradiction: 10:30–10:45 is order entry, and 10:45–11:00 is the matching window the TMS names PREOPEN.

This is the part most investors miss: your pre-open order does not just wait for the market. It helps set the opening price. A morning with heavy buying queued in pre-open will open higher than a morning with heavy selling queued — the opening price is the market’s first verdict, computed from the queue before a single live trade happens.

The fifteen minutes of matching are also why an order placed at 10:40 can still change your morning. Until the match runs, the order can be modified or cancelled; once the opening price is set, the orders that matched at it are trades, and the rest carry into the continuous session at their limit prices. What pre-open never does is give you a live price to chase: there is no ticking market in this window, only a queue and then a single computed open.

The trading day, 10:30 to 15:00: thirty minutes of pre-open, four hours of trading

04. 11:00–15:00 — Continuous trading: the four hours that count

At 11:00 the continuous session begins, and it runs for four hours, to 15:00. This is the session most people mean when they say “the market is open”. Orders match live, in real time, on price-time priority: the best price first, and at the same price, the earliest order first. The rule is written into NEPSE’s Securities Trading Operations Bylaws 2075, Rule 3(2).

Four hours sounds short next to global markets, and it is — but it is also the whole of it. The NEPSE index, the sector indices, the top gainers and the turnover figures all update tick by tick inside this window, and they freeze the moment it closes. Pre-open is 30 minutes against 240 minutes of continuous trading: one-eighth of the session, doing the job of setting the start.

Inside the four hours, the order book is the market. A market order takes the best price available right now; a limit order waits at the price you set, and fills only if the market comes to it — our market-depth explainer walks through how that queue works and why a fill can land away from the price on the screen. What the session does not do is protect you from your own order type: the difference between a market order and a limit order is decided when you place it, not when it fills.

05. After 15:00 — The bell rings. The queue doesn’t.

At 15:00 trading stops, and there is no after-hours trading on NEPSE. The close is the close. But the order book does not go home. Since 13 August 2026, the TMS accepts After Market Orders between 5:00 PM and 10:00 AM the next day, and lists the window as a session in its own right: AFTERMARKET, 17:00:00 to 10:00:00.

An After Market Order does not execute. It queues, and it is sent to the market when the next session opens. That is the whole point — and the whole limit. An order placed at 8 PM waits for the morning; it does not trade overnight, and it does not move the price while the market is closed. The window closes at 10:00 rather than running into pre-open at 10:30, so the queue is in place before the day’s first session begins.

The scale of the window is worth stating plainly: the queue is open for 17 hours, against four hours of continuous trading — more than four times as long. That is a convenience, not a market. Seventeen hours of order entry buys you a place in Monday’s queue, not a trade on Sunday night. The ability to queue orders outside market hours came from a SEBON-approved amendment to the Securities Trading Operations Bylaws 2075 in April 2026. Execution, though, still happens only inside session hours.

How to use the day

Three habits, one per session:

  1. Watch pre-open. The opening price shows the morning’s mood before a single trade. Orders only, no trades yet — read it as intent, not as a result.
  2. Trade in the session. A limit order caps the price you pay; a market order takes the best price available. Know which one you placed before you place it — and check what it costs: the trading-cost calculator works out broker commission, the SEBON fee, the DP charge and capital gains tax on a sale.
  3. Remember T+2. A trade made today settles two working days later — shares into the buyer’s Demat, money to the seller, on T+2. The trade is struck at 11:00; ownership moves on T+2.

What to take from it

“When does NEPSE open?” has three honest answers, and they are all times on the same day. The trading day opens at 10:30, when orders start queuing. The market opens at 11:00, when orders start matching live. And for an investor placing orders at night, the day effectively opens at 17:00 the evening before, when the after-market queue opens — with the one condition that matters: nothing in that queue is a trade until the morning’s sessions run.

The confusion is understandable, because the TMS itself lists four sessions and two of them never trade. The fix is to sort them by what they do, not by what they are called: two sessions take orders, one sets the open, one trades. Once that is clear, the rest follows — why a 10:40 order has not traded, why an 8 PM order has not moved by morning, and why the opening price, set in fifteen minutes of matching, is worth watching before the four hours that count.

Frequently asked questions

What time does NEPSE open?

Continuous trading opens at 11:00 AM and closes at 3:00 PM Nepal Standard Time, Monday to Friday. Pre-open runs 10:30 to 11:00 AM.

Can I place an order before 11:00 AM?

Yes. Orders can be placed in pre-open from 10:30 to 10:45 AM, and as After Market Orders between 5:00 PM and 10:00 AM. Neither executes until the market is open.

Do pre-open orders trade?

No. Pre-open orders are collected, then matched at 10:45–11:00 to set the opening price. No trade happens during pre-open itself — only the opening match.

Is there after-hours trading on NEPSE?

No. Trading stops at 3:00 PM. After Market Orders only queue for the next session; they do not execute outside market hours.

Why did my order placed at night not trade by morning?

Because an After Market Order is a queue, not a trade. It is sent to the market when the next session opens, and it fills only if a matching order is there at your price — under price-time priority, like any other order.

When do I actually own shares I bought today?

On T+2 — two working days after the trade date. The trade is struck in the continuous session; settlement follows.
