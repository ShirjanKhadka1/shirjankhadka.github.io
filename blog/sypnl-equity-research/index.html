SY Panel Nepal promised Rs 108.42 million in profit and delivered Rs 67.52 million. The market prices it at 259 times its earnings anyway.

SYPNL closed at Rs 1,334.10 on October 5, 2026, a 259 times multiple on verified earnings per share of Rs 5.16, against a manufacturing-peer median of 38.3 times. A blended valuation from the research desk lands at Rs 232, 82.6% below the price. A fundamentals-first institutional research note on what the premium is pricing in.

By Nepse Decode Research Desk · Kathmandu · October 10, 2026 · 10 min read
Facebook X LinkedIn WhatsApp Viber

SY Panel Nepal Limited closed at Rs 1,334.10 at NEPSE's Monday, October 5 session, a price that values the sandwich-panel maker at roughly 259 times its verified earnings per share of Rs 5.16. The earnings figure comes from the company's own Q4 FY 2082/83 filing, summarized by ShareSansar on August 14, 2026, and it reconciles cleanly: Rs 67.52 million of net profit over 13,081,371 listed shares. That single multiple is the whole story, and it is worth reading slowly, because nearly every other number in this note points the other way.

This is the research desk's first institutional equity research launch: a fundamentals-first note built on the company's filings published at synepal.com, the Prabhu Capital-hosted prospectus, ICRA Nepal's published rating actions, the NEPSE listing record at nepalstock.com, and market data as of the October 5, 2026 close. What follows is what those documents say, what they do not say, and the valuation they imply.

संक्षेपमा

एसवाई प्यानल नेपाल (नेप्से: SYPNL) ले आ.व. 2082/83 मा रु 6.75 करोड खुद नाफा कमायो, तर विवरणपत्रले रु 10.84 करोडको प्रक्षेपण गरेको थियो। अक्टोबर 5, 2026 मा शेयर मूल्य रु 1,334.10 छ, जुन पुष्टि भएको प्रति शेयर आम्दानी रु 5.16 को करिब 259 गुणा हो। उद्योगका अन्य कम्पनीहरूको मध्यक मूल्य-आम्दानी अनुपात 38.3 गुणा मात्र छ। अनुसन्धान टोलीको मिश्रित आधार अनुमान रु 232 छ, जुन बजार मूल्यभन्दा 82.6% कम हो। यो शैक्षिक उद्देश्यको अनुसन्धानमूलक विश्लेषण हो, लगानी सल्लाह होइन।

A small manufacturer with a Korean parent

SY Panel Nepal Limited is a manufacturer of EPS and PUF sandwich panels, UPVC windows, steel doors, and EPS materials for prefabricated construction. It was incorporated in July 2017 (ShareHub, Investopaper, ShareSansar, nepsestock, four sources in agreement) and runs its factory at Pithuwa, Ratnanagar-14, Chitwan, with a corporate office at Subidhanagar, Tinkune, Kathmandu. It is a subsidiary of South Korea's SY Co. Limited (KOSDAQ: 109610), which holds about 63% of the equity, with the rest of the promoter block held by Korean nationals associated with the parent.

The published capacity figures are identical across four sources, which is rare enough to note: about 5.38 million sq ft a year of sandwich panels, 6,000 UPVC windows a year, and 7,500 steel doors a year (Investopaper, September 2023; nepsestock; ShareHub; ShareSansar). The company sells through 62 plus dealers across Nepal (ShareSansar, September 2025; ShareHub) and is ISO 9001:2015 certified (ShareHub; the company's Q3 2082/83 filing).

The IPO is the reason this company is on NEPSE at all. It issued 5,232,548 shares at Rs 100, drawing about 2,129,715 applicants and an oversubscription of roughly 6.04 times, per CDSC data reported by ICT Frame. The general public tranche opened November 5, 2025 and allotted November 16, 2025 at Prabhu Capital, the issue manager. NEPSE listed 13,081,371 shares on December 9, 2025, trading began December 10, and the debut opened in a band of Rs 106.61 to Rs 319.83, per ShareSansar citing the NEPSE notice. The paid-up capital stands at Rs 1,308,137,100 at Rs 100 face, with promoters holding 60% and the public 40%.

The earnings the price ignores

The company's Q4 FY 2082/83 filing, summarized by ShareSansar on August 14, 2026, reports revenue from operations of Rs 812.79 million, up 7.50% year on year, and net profit of Rs 67.52 million, up just 1.08%. Earnings per share came in at Rs 5.16 and net worth per share at Rs 111.78, both reconciling arithmetically with the filing. The profit line barely moved while revenue grew because the equity base expanded 66.67% through the IPO: EPS nearly halved from Rs 8.51 the year before. This is dilution doing exactly what dilution does.

Then there is the prospectus. The Prabhu Capital-hosted prospectus projected FY 2082/83 net profit of Rs 108.42 million, with implied EPS of Rs 8.29 and a glide path to Rs 13.54 by FY 2084/85. Actual profit of Rs 67.52 million misses that projection by about 38%. A miss of that size in the first reported year after listing is not a rounding error; it is the company's own forecast failing against its own first scorecard.

The filing has brighter corners. Trade receivables fell 32.27% while revenue rose 7.5%, which reads as improved collection. But the company's own Q3 filing booked a foreign-exchange translation loss of Rs 13.79 million and its management commentary conceded that profitability was "influenced by significant foreign exchange volatility," with steel coils and chemicals imported partly from the Korean parent. And on distributions, the record is blank: no dividend, no bonus, no rights, no auction to date, per ShareSansar's company record and Nepali Paisa's "Recent Dividend: -".

What peers charge for the same earnings

Set SYPNL against six operating manufacturers in the same NEPSE sector, on Nepali Paisa's Q4 FY 2082/83 fundamentals with closes from the October 5 session, and the premium is stark. Shivam Cements changes hands at 47.90 times earnings, Himalayan Distillery at 39.19 times, Sonapur Minerals at 37.49 times, Unilever Nepal at 28.62 times, and Bottlers Nepal at 31.51 times on its October 2 close, since it did not trade on October 5. Reliance Spinning Mills, a February 2026 listing still in its own hype cycle, trades at 127.67 times. The median of the six is 38.34 times. SYPNL, at about 259 times on the verified EPS of Rs 5.16, costs 5.5 to 6.7 times the median multiple, and even against Reliance Spinning it is roughly twice as rich. On book value the story is the same: SYPNL at 11.94 times book against a peer median of 4.72 times, or about 2.5 times richer.

Two caveats keep this honest. First, ShareHub shows an EPS of Rs 6.28 for SYPNL against Nepali Paisa's Rs 5.16, both labeled Q4 FY 2082/83, and that conflict is unresolved; the research desk used the verified 5.16 throughout, since it reconciles with the filed profit. Second, Unilever Nepal and Bottlers Nepal are functionally illiquid, with 30 and 10 shares changing hands on their last sessions, so their multiples carry stale-price risk. Neither caveat changes the shape: the peer-implied fair values are Rs 197.83 on earnings and Rs 527.04 on book, a band of Rs 198 to Rs 527 that sits 60 to 85% below the market price.

The DCF leg

Multiples describe; a discounted cash flow explains. The research desk built a conventional 10-year free-cash-flow-to-firm model with a Gordon terminal value, stating the adaptation explicitly: no licence-expiry, no-PPA template applies to a going-concern manufacturer. The weighted average cost of capital comes out at 10.65%, from a risk-free rate of 2.60% anchored to NRB's 91-day T-bill auctions plus a tenor uplift, a Nepal equity risk premium of 8.89% from Damodaran's country risk premiums, a bottom-up beta of 0.964 built from Damodaran's Building Materials unlevered beta of 0.89 relevered at 10% debt, and a pre-tax cost of debt of 8.00% anchored to commercial-bank base rates plus a spread for the sub-investment-grade issuer. Nepali Paisa's single-source beta of 2.11, built on under ten months of data with undisclosed methodology, was rejected as unreliable.

The model passes its own consistency check: at an assumed 70% capacity utilization and stated realizations, capacity-implied FY 2082/83 revenue is Rs 808.29 million against the filed Rs 812.79 million, a difference of 0.55%. Three scenarios follow. The base case, a gradual utilization recovery with the UPVC plant commissioned in FY 2084/85, values the equity at Rs 67.76 a share. The bear case, where the prospectus miss persists and the plant slips a year, gives Rs 17.70. The bull case, a construction upcycle with successful import substitution, gives Rs 161.86. Even the bull case sits below the peer-implied band.

The target blends 50% DCF with 30% of the P/B-implied Rs 527.04 and 20% of the P/E-implied Rs 197.83, using the verified EPS. The arithmetic lands at Rs 207 for the bear case, Rs 232 for the base case, and Rs 279 for the bull case: implied returns of negative 84.5%, negative 82.6%, and negative 79.1% against the Rs 1,334.10 close. The desk's rating rule is mechanical, buy at plus 20% or better, hold between negative 10% and plus 20%, reduce between negative 30% and negative 10%, sell below negative 30%, and it is applied without softening. On the base target, the implied negative 82.6% reads SELL. That is the research desk's view, reported as fact, not as an instruction to any reader.

The parent paradox

The strangest line in this note belongs to the parent. SY Co. Limited of Korea, which holds about 63% of SY Panel Nepal, reported FY2025 revenue of KRW 501.55 billion against a net loss of KRW 14.13 billion and negative operating cash flow of KRW 15.28 billion, per stockanalysis.com, a third-party aggregator. Finnhub puts the parent's ten-year price return at negative 91.57%. Back in January 2026, laganilab noted the paradox plainly: the parent's market capitalization, roughly NPR 12 to 14 billion, sat below the Nepal subsidiary's then market value, which stood near Rs 17.45 billion at the October 5 close. The child, priced at 259 times earnings, is worth more than the bleeding parent.

That structure carries a concrete tension. The promoter block of 60% is locked until November 2028, a date carried by ShareHub and only partially corroborated, by laganilab's "late 2028" and the three-year arithmetic from the November 16, 2025 allotment, so the desk treats it as provisional. When the lock-in lifts, a loss-making parent holding a 63% stake in a company priced at nearly 12 times book has an obvious incentive to monetize part of it, and the 40% free float is thin enough that any sell-down would press hard on the price. ICRA Nepal's issuer rating of [ICRANP-IR] BB+, upgraded one notch on September 24, 2025 per ShareSansar's coverage, is a moderate-risk opinion, sub-investment grade, and it prices the credit accordingly.

The UPVC bet

The bull case for this company is poured concrete, not spreadsheet optimism. On April 15, 2026, the company laid the foundation stone for S.Y. U.P.V.C Profile Pvt. Ltd., a Rs 56 crore UPVC profile plant at its existing Ratnanagar-14, Pithuwa site, in a ceremony attended by the Ratnanagar mayor and the parent company's CEO (ICT Frame; the company's own YouTube channel). The company's Q2 FY 2082/83 filing confirms Rs 390 million of IPO proceeds already invested in the subsidiary, and the balance-sheet investment line jumped from Rs 42.5 million to Rs 432.5 million to match. The company's own rationale is vertical integration: a proprietary supply chain for its prefabricated housing materials, shielding margins from external price volatility, plus sales into a UPVC market heavily reliant on imports. Nepal's boards, sheets, and panels market is 70 to 85% import-dependent, per IndexBox, so the substitution logic is real.

But it is a bet at foundation stage, with no announced commissioning date, no disclosed funding split beyond the Rs 390 million of IPO equity, and a project cost equal to about 69% of annual revenue. The company has no track record of executing a project of this scale. In the DCF, the bear case slips commissioning by a year and the base case lands it in FY 2084/85; both are assumptions, labeled as such, because the company has not published a date.

The price action

The tape tells its own story across 192 trading sessions. The stock debuted December 10, 2025 and hit the 10% upper circuit on seven of its first eight sessions, on tiny volumes. It gained 175.23% in its partial debut month, then 100.41% in January 2026, touching an intraday high of Rs 2,255.90 on January 27 (ShareHub), the all-time high. From there it has drifted down 40.86% to the October 5 close of Rs 1,334.10. The 52-week low of Rs 319.80 is the debut session's open; the stock has never traded below it.

At the close, the 20-day simple moving average sat at Rs 1,323.41, exactly matching ShareSansar's published MA20, with the 50-day at Rs 1,317.05, the RSI14 at 53.13 for neutral, and the MACD line at 10.89 above its signal at 7.22, short-term positive but small relative to the price. The price rides 0.8% above its 20-day average: no meaningful trend deviation. Liquidity is genuine by NEPSE standards despite the 40% float, averaging 44,620 shares and Rs 59.62 million a day over the last 63 sessions, and SYPNL has repeatedly topped the daily turnover chart, including Rs 30.98 crore on December 23, 2025 and Rs 59.29 crore on February 3, 2026, per ShareSansar's daily summaries. With only 192 bars of history, a 200-day average and twelve-month statistics do not exist; the desk states that plainly rather than extrapolating.

What the files do not yet say

Several items sit outside what the sources confirm, and this note keeps them there. The EPS conflict between ShareHub's 6.28 and Nepali Paisa's 5.16 is unresolved, pending the audited annual report; the desk used 5.16 because it reconciles with the filed profit, and notes that resolving at 6.28 would lift the base target only to about Rs 240, immaterial to the rating. No full audited four-year financial series could be built: the annual report PDF sits behind the company's bot verification and the auditor's name is unpublished. The promoter lock-in unlock date of November 2028 is single-sourced. The UPVC plant's commissioning date is undisclosed. The parent's figures come from third-party aggregators, not official filings, and the peer fundamentals are single-sourced from aggregators. None of these gaps flatters the price; all of them are stated so the reader can weigh them.

The unresolved question

Put the two pictures together and the tension is plain. On one side: a real manufacturer with verified capacity, 62 plus dealers, ISO certification, improving collections, and a Rs 56 crore vertical-integration bet already under construction, in an import-dependent market with genuine growth tailwinds. On the other: Rs 67.52 million of profit against a Rs 108.42 million promise, earnings per share halved by dilution, zero distributions to shareholders, a sub-investment-grade issuer rating, a loss-making Korean parent, and a price of Rs 1,334.10 that charges 259 times verified earnings in a sector where the median multiple is 38.3.

Either the premium is the market paying early for a construction-materials franchise before the UPVC plant lands, in which case 259 times is foresight, or the filings are telling the truth about a small manufacturer whose own forecast failed in year one, in which case the price is expectation alone. The numbers that would decide it, a full audited annual report, a commissioning date for the plant, and a few quarters of post-IPO earnings, do not exist yet. What breaks the premium, if anything does, will be one of those facts arriving, not the chart. Until then, the Rs 1,334 question stays open.

A note on what is verified here. Every figure above comes from a named source: the company's Q2, Q3, and Q4 FY 2082/83 filings published at synepal.com (revenue Rs 812.79 million, net profit Rs 67.52 million, EPS Rs 5.16, BVPS Rs 111.78, receivables down 32.27%, FX translation loss Rs 13.79 million, Rs 390 million invested in the UPVC subsidiary, the prospectus profit projections of Rs 108.42 million for FY 2082/83), ShareSansar's coverage of those filings and of the NEPSE listing record, ICT Frame on the IPO subscription and the UPVC foundation ceremony, the SEBON pipeline record of the 2080/06/19 IPO application, ICRA Nepal's September 24, 2025 upgrade to [ICRANP-IR] BB+ as reported by ShareSansar, laganilab's January 2026 analysis of the parent paradox, stockanalysis.com and Finnhub for parent-company figures, and IndexBox for the import-dependence estimate. Market figures carry the October 5, 2026 session stamp from Nepali Paisa, ShareSansar, and ShareHub.
