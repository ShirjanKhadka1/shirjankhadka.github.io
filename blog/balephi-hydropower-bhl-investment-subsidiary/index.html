Balephi is building an investment company with shareholder money. The notice does not say what it will buy

Balephi Hydropower has registered BHL Investment Limited, a public company with Rs 50 crore in authorized capital and Rs 2.5 crore issued. Its board approved paying Rs 1 crore of a Rs 2 crore commitment as share capital. The disclosure names the money, not the mission.

By Shirjan Khadka · Analyst, Nepse Decode, Kathmandu · October 4, 2026 · 4 min read
Facebook X LinkedIn WhatsApp Viber

Balephi Hydropower’s disclosure of BHL Investment share capital to NEPSE on October 4, 2026 is brief and fully official: the company has registered BHL Investment Limited, a public company, with the Office of the Company Registrar for investment objectives, and its board has approved the first share-capital payment into it. The official notice, signed by company secretary Pabina Sapkota and dated 2083/06/15, is the kind of disclosure investors should read twice, because it answers how much and when while leaving why and into what completely open.

The figures in the notice are precise. BHL Investment Limited carries an authorized capital of Rs 50,00,00,000, fifty crore rupees, and an issued capital of Rs 2,50,00,000, two crore fifty lakh. Balephi Hydropower committed Rs 2,00,00,000, two crore, as equity investment in the new company. On 2083/06/02, BHL Investment requested Rs 1,00,00,000, one crore, as share-capital payment, and Balephi’s board meeting of 2083/06/15 decided to pay exactly that amount.

Put the numbers next to each other and the shape of the plan emerges. The Rs 1 crore paid now is half of Balephi’s Rs 2 crore commitment, so the remaining Rs 1 crore of that commitment sits unpaid, on a timeline the notice does not give. The Rs 2 crore commitment is 80 percent of the new company’s Rs 2.5 crore issued capital, which makes Balephi the dominant shareholder of its own subsidiary. And Rs 2.5 crore issued is just 5 percent of the Rs 50 crore authorized capital, so the subsidiary can call up to twenty times its current capital without any fresh resolution on authorization. The authorized capital is a ceiling, not a plan, but ceilings matter: it tells you the scale the founders were thinking about when they registered the company.

The question the notice raises is strategic, and it is the one the notice does not answer. Balephi Hydropower is the developer of the 36 MW Upper Balephi ‘A’ Hydropower Project; its letterhead says so. A hydropower company exists to build and operate power plants, and deploying shareholder equity into an investment vehicle is a different business entirely. The stated purpose of the new company is investment objectives, in the company’s own words, and that is the whole of the mandate on record: no target sectors, no expected returns, no deployment timeline, no named co-investors for the rest of the issued capital.

To be fair to the disclosure, it is complete where it counts procedurally. It is addressed to the Nepal Stock Exchange, and copied to SEBON, to CDS and Clearing Limited, and to Himalayan Investment Banker Limited as share registrar. The commitment, the payment request, and the board decision are all dated and signed. Investors are not being asked to trust a rumor; they are reading a board decision, published through the official channel the same day the board took it.

What remains unresolved is the substance. When BHL Investment calls the remaining Rs 1 crore of Balephi’s commitment, and into what assets it deploys the Rs 1 crore already paid, will have to surface in future disclosures, because this notice covers only the payment. A Rs 2 crore equity commitment is small next to the capital of a 36 MW project, so the immediate financial exposure is limited; the significance is directional. A listed hydro developer has formally entered the investment business, and the market will now watch what its new company does with the money. Until those disclosures arrive, the file on Balephi’s diversification holds four verified numbers and one open question.
