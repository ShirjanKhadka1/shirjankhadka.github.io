Verdict Engine v2 · Methodology

How the warning radar is built

Every figure on the Warning Radar is produced by tools/build-verdicts-v2.js running the engine in tools/verdict-engine-v2/ — the coded rules in rules.js are the single source of truth. This page states every rule, weight, gate, and known limitation exactly as coded, so any verdict can be traced and reproduced. Educational research — not investment advice. Past patterns do not predict future results.

In plain words: the engine looks at how each stock's price and trading volume moved over time, hunting for the classic signs of a pump-and-dump — sudden limit-up runs, prices stretched far above normal, heavy selling hidden under excitement. Each sign gets a weight, the weights add up to a risk score, and the score decides the label you see. Everything below is the exact, unedited rulebook — written for anyone who wants to check our work.
The honest frame, first: the engine flagged 89.4% of historical pump episodes 5 sessions before the peak — and it still misses some, and its flags are not trade instructions. As a mechanical trading system it adds zero edge over buy-and-hold (measured). Its proven value is telling you which names are dangerous and why.

1. What the engine outputs

Each symbol receives one verdict label, two scores, and a confidence value — plus an evidence bundle (circuit record, volume anatomy, factor readings, fundamentals context) shown on every expanded row.

  • pump_score (0–1): weighted composite of six scored bearish flags. Higher means more pump-and-distribution footprints active.
  • opportunity_score (0–1): weighted composite of two scored bullish flags (ignition, bounce setup).
  • confidence: 0.35 base + 0.10 per agreeing scored flag, capped at 0.90. Always below 1 — never overstated. Experimental flags never count.
  • Verdict labels (as coded): DISTRIBUTION_WARNING when pump_score ≥ 0.60 or ≥ 2 bearish flags are active; ELEVATED_RISK when pump_score ≥ 0.35; EXHAUSTION_RISK when the circuit-exhaustion flag is active; IGNITION when listing age ≤ 10 with an active tick-aware circuit run; BOUNCE_SETUP when bounce criteria are met; NEUTRAL when clean; UNRELIABLE when the data itself is poisoned (rule 11); EXCLUDED_NON_EQUITY for debentures, mutual funds and promoter-share lines (rule 4); INSUFFICIENT_DATA when there are fewer than 30 sessions and no dossier. The 0.60 / 0.35 cutoffs are operational choices, not research-derived.

2. Data

  • Source: daily OHLCV per NEPSE symbol from the open samirwagle/Nepse-All-Scraper dataset (MIT licence), fetched at build time per symbol. Price history is never republished — only the derived verdicts are.
  • Window: at most the most recent 750 sessions per symbol (the research mirror window the engine was validated on), so ancient unadjusted corporate-action bars cannot poison the data-quality gate.
  • Session date: the authoritative trading day comes from live.json session_date — never from fetch timestamps. NEPSE trades Monday–Friday; a weekend pipeline refresh must never stamp a weekend date.
  • Official close merge: after market close, the validated official quotes are appended as the latest bar when the fetched history has not ingested the new session yet. The official feed never publishes the session open — it stays 0 (never invented).
  • Market regime: computed from the site's own index daily history — bull if close > SMA200 and SMA200 rising, bear if close < SMA200 and SMA200 falling, else sideways.

4. Circuit detection and the age gates

  • NEPSE circuit limits: ±10% before 2026-04-20, ±15% from 2026-04-20.
  • A bar counts as a limit-up day at 0.999 of the limit (canonical); the ignition run uses a looser 0.995 tick-aware tolerance — genuine circuits print 9.97–10.03%.
  • Age gates (rule 1): listing age ≤ 10 → bullish ignition weight; age 11–14 → transition, circuit signal suppressed (no signal); age ≥ 15 → bearish exhaustion weight. Both sides rest on 57 listings at p<0.01 — core weights are not promoted without out-of-sample confirmation.
  • If the most recent limit-up was more than 30 sessions ago, the circuit weight decays ×0.5 (stale-window guard).

5. Honesty gates

  • Regime gates (rule 2): circuit, markup_asym and vol_surge are scored in bull regimes only (they invert sideways); slowbleed_v2 scores in bear regimes only and stays experimental — never in the composite; mom20 and turnover_spike are regime-independent. When the regime is unknown, gated factors are shown but not scored — honest abstention, not a guess.
  • Sector multipliers (rule 6): Finance and Development Bank → bearish factor weights ×0 (a CONTRADICTORY_SECTOR note is emitted; readings are shown, not scored); Hydropower ×0.5; every other equity sector ×1.0.
  • Excluded instruments (rule 4): Debentures, Mutual Funds and Promoter Shares receive EXCLUDED_NON_EQUITY — NAV/par-pegged tapes make momentum and markup meaningless and institutional block crosses trigger false distribution flags.
  • Volume floors (rule 5): a limit-up day counts only on ≥500 shares (young listings ≤10 exempt — genuine ignitions print tiny); vol_surge needs a 120-day median baseline ≥1,000 shares; a turnover-spike signal day needs ≥2,000 shares. Odd-lot artifacts are not climaxes.
  • Late-circuit fade (rule 7): limit-up within the last 5 sessions AND 20-day return below −15% → bearish flag. Post-peak limit-ups during bleeds consistently failed (SAIL, RFPL, CITY, SHIVM; SOHL the lone exception).
  • Hydro cliff (rule 8): a limit-down on less than 0.5× median volume is an info-level "absent bids" bearish acceleration note, not a scored flag — tops break from absent bids, not selling pressure (e.g. DORDI 4,637; BHL 5,247; KKHC 26,118 shares on limit-down cliffs).
  • Event suppressions (rule 9): volume-spike flags are suppressed on 2024-03-04 (market-wide dead-volume day) and 2026-07-13/14 (cross-debenture institutional rebalance) — those were market-wide artifacts, not single-name signal.
  • Data-quality poison (rule 11): any single-day |return| above 25% is impossible under any circuit regime → verdict UNRELIABLE, factors not scored. The engine refuses to score rather than guess.
  • Withheld float: sub-1,000-share days on a large (Rs 1b+ paid-up, an engine choice) young listing (≤60 sessions) is an info-level ignition-precondition note. The measured version — traded public float below 1.0% of the public float with active ignition — is experimental and shown as unbacktested evidence only.
  • Short-horizon lenses: for listings under 60 sessions, experimental 20/10-day volume lenses are computed for diagnosis but never enter the core score.

6. Lock-in dates — the hard rule

The engine never publishes a lock-in expiry computed from a price-series-start proxy as fact. A precise expiry is shown only from verified sources: Shirjan-supplied corrections (user-verified) or the Capital Max IPO lock-in table (lockin-table). Proxy-derived dates carry the explicit note "computed from price-series start; true IPO date pending". The reference example: our series-start proxy implied a 2026-10-11 expiry for HATHY; Shirjan's correction showed true listing around 2023-09-29/30 with lock-in already expired — nearly two weeks earlier. Verdict semantics differ completely between "expiry approaching" and "promoters already free to sell".

7. Fundamentals context (evidence only, never scored)

Point-in-time company-quality context is attached as evidence only: EPS (TTM), ROE (TTM), cash dividend %, BVPS, P/E (TTM), net profit. Research finding: pumped new listings do not have weak fundamentals — they have absent fundamentals (75% no EPS vs 10% market). Missing earnings on a young listing with ignition/pump activity is a first-class risk input ("no anchor"); where earnings exist, P/E (TTM) above 100 is flagged as an extreme-multiple note. Historical quarterly fundamentals are unavailable, so no trend scoring is attempted.

10. Known limitations

  • The 0.60 / 0.35 verdict cutoffs are operational choices, not research-derived.
  • Values marked [ENGINE CHOICE] in the code (e.g. the Rs 1b "large paid-up" line) are engine-parameterized, not research-derived.
  • The cornered-float snapshot has no history to backtest against; fundamentals are point-in-time with no quarterly history; the engine was validated on ≤750-session histories.
  • Every research value is cited to the document that produced it; hand-tuned parameters are labeled as such. The engine never emits buy/sell calls and never claims to be a market-beating system.
