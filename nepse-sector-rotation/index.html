How this map is built

The benchmark and the sectors. Each sector is a derived index built by Nepse Decode: the equal-weighted chained daily returns of the sector's listed companies, computed from our own OHLC archive (the same series behind the Seasonality and Market Regime pages). The benchmark is the official NEPSE index. Sector membership is today's map applied historically, so delisted names are absent — a survivorship caveat we state rather than hide.

The quadrant chart. The chart at the top is a daily mechanism with the construction stated on it: 20-day relative strength, 5-day smoothed. For each sector, the relative-strength line (sector index ÷ NEPSE index) is measured over the last 20 sessions: x = that 20-session change in percent, smoothed with a 5-session exponential average; y = how much the smoothed value itself moved over the last 5 sessions, in percentage points. Quadrants follow the signs of x and y. Sessions are counted on the grid of sessions present in both the sector series and the official index history. Two honest differences from the best-known version of this chart: our frame always includes every sector (theirs clips dots outside a fixed frame — on 2026-10-16 their Banking and Hotels dots sit outside it), and our sectors are equal-weighted composites rather than cap-weighted sub-indices, so our weak-sector dots sit further left. Checked against that chart's published 2026-10-16 dots, our relative-strength signs agree on all 7 readable sectors and our quadrants on 5 of 7 — Non Life Insurance and Life Insurance land one quadrant apart because the final sessions moved our equal-weighted baskets differently. Their twelfth dot, Trading, is folded into our Others group and has no separate dot here.

RS-Ratio (the table). The weekly posture table is built on weekly closes — the last session of each week. RS = sector index ÷ NEPSE index at each weekly close. RS-Ratio = 100 × (RS ÷ its own trailing 13-week average − 1), in percent. Above 0, the sector's relative line is running above its own 13-week trend — it has been beating the index by more than its recent norm; below 0, it is running below that trend. RS-Momentum = RS-Ratio minus its own trailing 4-week average: above 0, the ratio has been rising over the last four weeks. Weekly bars are deliberate: measured weekly, relative strength rotates smoothly from quadrant to quadrant; measured daily, the same maths whipsaws around every sharp move. Every input is our own data and the formulas are published, not borrowed as a black box.

Quadrants. Leading (ratio ≥ 0, momentum ≥ 0): strong and still strengthening. Weakening (≥ 0, < 0): strong but losing steam. Lagging (both < 0): weak and still weakening. Improving (< 0, ≥ 0): weak but turning up — tomorrow's leaders often enter here. Weeks in Quad counts consecutive weeks in the current quadrant; Consistency is the share of the last 30 weeks spent in the sector's most frequent quadrant. Rotation rank orders sectors by RS-Ratio + RS-Momentum, their summed distance from the 0 / 0 centre.

Turnover and flow shift. Turnover % is the sector's share of total turnover across sector-mapped companies in the latest session (exchange-reported turnover from our archive). Flow Shift compares that share with its trailing 120-session average, in percentage points: positive means money is rotating toward the sector relative to its recent norm, negative means it is rotating away.

Reading it honestly. Rotation is relative: a sector can sit in Leading while falling, if it falls less than the index, and a Lagging sector can rise if it rises less. The map shows where relative strength and money are moving — it is a map, not a signal, and it says nothing about whether any sector is cheap or dear.

Mechanical relative-strength statistics for education, not investment advice. Data: Nepse Decode derived sector indices + official NEPSE index history, rebuilt after each close. Sector history begins when each derived index has enough sessions (Banking 2011, Hydropower 2009, Investment 2020).
